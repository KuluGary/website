During the summer break of one of my last years of middle-school, the phone rang. One of my friends wanted to get together and make a videogame.

At that point in time I already loved playing videogames, but I didn’t know you could *make them yourself*. I excitedly said yes and my group of friends and I started going to the library with my laptop and got to design a game. “Design a game” may be an exaggeration… at first, we just drew characters and levels in a notebook, and after that we spent hours deciding what game engine to use.

I remember I had a Dragon Ball book, and in that book there was a very small passage about how people were making Dragon Ball games using the MUGEN engine.

> […] With the “M.U.G.E.N.” program (a 2D fighting game “maker”, which allows importing fighters and giving them any attributes we want), fans have created a fighting game based on “Dragon Ball AF”, where you can select Super Saiyan 5 Goku or Super Saiyan 4 Gohan […].

My friends and I downloaded it and “made some games” (in reality, we just downloaded the engine and some characters people already created…). We had fun, but using ready-made assets didn’t feel like “making a game”. After that I vividly remember we went to the mall, and downloaded Blender on McDonald’s wi-fi but the UI was so confusing we couldn’t even delete the default cube.

Of course, summer break came and went and we never even made anything close to a game, but since then I got hooked.

At some point, I downloaded RPG Maker XP. At the time, it was one of the newest and most popular no-code game engines available. I loved how easy it was to make something you could play and share, since it already had a lot of pre-installed assets such as sprites and music.

{% image category, page.fileSlug %}
rpg-maker-xp.png | RPG Maker had a very advanced visual editor for the time. | max-w-half
{% endimage %}

I started off making Pokémon fangames. There was this framework built on top of RPG Maker that allowed making Pokémon games very close to the originals, including the capturing mechanics and such. I played around with that for a long while, but after that I started making my own original games.


One of the first “original” games I made was a classic fantasy JRPG. It used a lot of the default RPG Maker assets, and it had a very long intro explaining the backstory of the world. I even released a short demo for it in one of the game dev forums I frequented.

After that I didn’t game dev for a while, until I found Game Maker 8 at a sale and decided to get it. At that point I’ve never written a line of code, so going from something like RPG Maker to Game Maker was a big jump. Even though Game Maker had a “blueprint”-like system, I decided I wanted to learn to code so at that point I started watching lots of tutorials.
