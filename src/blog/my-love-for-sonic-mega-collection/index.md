Today, you can get most of Sonic the Hedgehog’s classic games in one of the bajillion compilations SEGA has released, both runs of the comics at an online book retailer, and concept art or game illustrations in any of the many well-maintained fan communities.

In the early 2000s, with limited access to the Internet and only the biggest names being imported from overseas, you could only really play the games in original hardware and find art in some specialized stores.

That’s why I remember one day going to the game store at the mall, and finding Sonic Mega Collection while browsing the used games bin. At that point in time I’d already played Sonic games, both in the GameCube and older systems such as the Mega Drive and Game Gear, but it had been a while since and this collection included games I’d never even heard of.

The biggest highlight for me is probably the music. After more than 20 years since I bought the game, I still go back to listening the soundtrack from time to time. Specially the Extras section theme, which got ingrained into my brain from how much I stared at the Archie Comics’ cover art and game illustrations.

Speaking of the Extras, much of what they shared I hadn’t seen before. I didn’t even know there were Sonic the Hedgehog comic books. I spent countless hours just looking at the covers and imagining what stories there would be in those issues.
