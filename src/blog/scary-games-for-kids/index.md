I have a soft spot for scary games made for kids.

Videogames as a medium are not strangers to horror. The market is full of well-designed, really scary games. Anything from classic franchises like *Resident Evil*, *Silent Hill* or *Rule of Rose* to modern gems like *Amnesia* are fantastic examples with tons of fans across the globe.

But there are other kinds of media with “horror” in it that is targeted to a different kind of audience. Book series like *Goosebumps*, television shows like *Courage the Cowardly Dog* or even the modern-day interpretation of *Halloween* are a way to make “horror for kids” palatable and enjoyable.

My first time encountering something like this in a videogame was the original *Luigi’s Mansion* for the GameCube.

Luigi’s Mansion is designed with a kid’s funfair “haunted house” look and feel in mind. While there are no jumpscares in it, there definitely is a creepyness which enhances the atmosphere of the game. For example, there’s this section where all the lights in the mansion go out, and there’s an unnerving quiet while Luigi walks around the darkened hallways, muttering and whimpering and trying to survive the ghosts that spawn all around. It’s a standout moment in the game, and the creepiest part of it for a lot of people.

There are a lot of other games with spooky elements in them. It’s been a popular motif since very early on, and lots of kids games have them. Kingdom Hearts has *Halloween Town*, Mario has had *Haunted House* levels since forever, Digimon World has *Grey Lord’s Mansion*, *Sonic the Hedgehog* has tons of halloween-y levels… Spooky-themed levels or characters have been a staple of too many games to count.

Another way to expose children to horror themes is how the *The Legend of Zelda* franchise approaches it. *The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time* has an infamous dungeon called *The Bottom of the Well*, where children all over the world got spooked by the end-boss being out-of-the-blue creepy and gross. The sequel to *Ocarina*, though, took things to the next level. While it’s a fun and hopeful game at its core, there’s also a contrast of deep sadness and eeriness that makes it a unique experience. Just the premise is a good example: “it is predicted that on the dawn of Termina’s festival, the Moon will crash into Clock Town and obliterate all life”. If nothing else, kid-me would be freaked-out by the ever-present grimacing Moon inching closer with each passing day.

But not only old games have taken horror as a setting. 2026’s *Mina the Hollower* is a homage to GameBoy Color-era Zelda games crossed with a Victorian horror setting and atmosphere, with cartoon-y horror enemies and locations sprinkled around. UNDERTALE and DELTARUNE take the “creepy-pasta” concept that became popular for older games and uses it to enhance their meta-narrative. “Mascot horror” is a sub-genre of horror games where the horror comes from “mascot” characters –game mascots like Sonic the Hedgehog or Bubsy the Cat –being full-on slasher-type entities, popularized by games like *Five Nights at Freddy’s*.

One of the most popular and maybe unexpected games that could be classified as “scary” is *Minecraft*. No matter how you look at it, Minecraft is not a horror game and does not have any overtly horror themes aside from some enemy designs, but whomever has been online around 2010 has seen the urban legends born from this game. There’s something about this game’s slow-paced gameplay, low resolution graphics and quiet and lonely atmosphere that has triggered so many people’s imagination.

In my case, while I loved the “spooky” themed levels and games, there was one time it backfired on me. When I was a kid, we rented Pikmin 1 from our home video rental store, and since I was young and didn’t quite get the mechanics, night-time came and all my Pikmin died in the end-of-day cutscene. Needless to say, I got really scared and sad and we returned the game without playing it again. Today, Pikmin is one of my favorite franchises but kid-me would not want anything to do with it for at least another ten years.
