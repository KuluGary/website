So, I recently entered my first game jam and got second place.

A couple of weeks ago [molegato announced on Bluesky](https://bsky.app/profile/molegato.com/post/3mwqkouajtc2p) he’d be hosting a game jam for [Halloween](https://molegato.com/halloween-gamejam-2026/). I’ve always been reluctant to enter game jams; I’d say to myself I didn’t have the time or that I already had enough deadlines as-is, but the timing on this one was unusually good so I decided I’d make something simple for the fun of it.

My entry was called “[Theodore’s Great Hunt](https://kulugary.itch.io/theodores-great-hunt)”.

If you want to play it, please let me know what you think of it! Otherwise, I’ll talk about my process for a little bit.

The idea was to parody “Duck Hunt” for the NES, and the art-style was inspired by Game Boy-era graphics and sounds. The end result is a very short game, with only three levels and around 10 minutes of game time.

Even though I have more experience coding, I wanted to spend most of my time on art and music so I could gain more experience in those areas. This was my first time composing a game soundtrack by myself, so I spent a lot of time fiddling around on my DAW.

I think both goods and bads of the game come from the fact that it’s a very short game. Something I decided from the beginning is that I wanted keep the scope small, and to submit a fully finished game no matter what.

With this in mind, I was able to make a complete package with a full game-loop, new graphics and music for each level and the simple gameplay also meant I could attain a good level of polish… But it also meant that there’s not “much to it” in this game.

{% image section=category, slug=postSlug %}
course-1.png | Course 1 level transition | | pixelated
{% endimage %}

The simplicity and short duration makes for a game that is not very “engaging”. There’s not a lot of variation in enemy patterns, and the difficulty curve is barely noticeable. When you are getting into it, it suddenly ends. I feel like all of these things prevent it for being a better experience for the player.

With all of this, I’m really glad I participated. I learnt a lot that I wouldn’t if I just kept prototyping forever, gave me a more holistic look into game development, and also let me play a bunch of cool games made by other devs!

Please check out all other the devs out, and play their games! You can find all of them [here](https://molegato.com/halloween-gamejam-2026/).

Let’s see if I sign up for another game jam in the future.
