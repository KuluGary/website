When we bought the VHS tape for this movie, I must have already seen bits and pieces of Digimon Adventure and Digimon Adventure 02 on TV. I remember putting it in the VHS player and being absolutely baffled when this showed up on screen…

{% image category, page.fileSlug %}

{% endimage %}

I thought “we must have bought a bootleg”! To this day, I don’t know what or who is Angela Anaconda… Anyway, right after that the actual Digimon: The Movie started playing, thankfully.

The Digimon: The Movie experience is very different than watching the TV show. Originally, there were three short films that were combined to make one full-feature film to sell overseas: *Digimon Adventure*, *Digimon Adventure: Our War Game!* and *Digimon Adventure 02: Hurricane Touchdown!!*.

I’ll talk about them separately:

## Digimon Adventure

{% image category, page.fileSlug %}
digimon-adventure.png
{% endimage %}

This movie is set before the events of the first TV Show, and depicts the “first contact” of the protagonists with digital monsters.

This short film is a contender for my favorite of the three. While the TV show has a nice “shōnen cartoon” feel to it, where the Digimon are companions and the power of friendship wins, this movies shows a different side of Digimon that fascinated me as a kid.

At around the half-way point of the movie, Koromon evolves into Agumon. Instead of the dorky character from the TV Show, this version is more “monster-like”. It acts like a wild animal, can’t talk, and promptly escapes from the protagonist’s house into the city. The ambiance of night-time Tokyo in this sequence is phenomenal, and when a parrot-like Digimon appears in the sky to cause havoc, the kaijū battle between it and Greymon left a deep impression on me.

In the end, this movie and the Digimon World game are what made me like more animalistic depictions of Digimon, probably.

## Digimon Adventure: Our War Game!

{% image category, page.fileSlug %}
our-war-game.png
{% endimage %}

The other contender for my favourite short film of the three. The movie is set after the events of Digimon Adventure.

In this movie, the protagonists have to fight a Digimon that has been infected with an Internet virus, and is taking control of real-world communications. Unlike in the show, the protagonists are not physically fighting alongside the Digimon, and instead they have to watch through a computer screen.

The depiction of the Internet in this movie is very cool. The abstract backgrounds contrast with the TV show more techno-naturalistic look, and makes it feel like “not a real place”. Alongside that, the spider-like antagonist fits very well with the concept of a “digital virus”.

The final sequence of the fight, were the protagonist’s Digimon evolve into their combined form thanks to their fight being broadcasted to the entire world through the web is a really exciting moment, using the setting of the Internet to deliver a very climactic moment.

## Digimon Adventure 02: Hurricane Touchdown!!

{% image category, page.fileSlug %}
hurricane-touchdown.png
{% endimage %}

This one is kinda weird. I was always less attached to the 02 cast of characters, and while the movie is as well made as the previous ones in term of animation, the story didn’t click as much with me. From what I know, it seems this short movie is the one that was most affected by the editing while converting it into a full-length film, so maybe that’s why.

Following the previous movie, Wendigomon is a Digimon that was corrupted by the same virus that took over in Our War Game! It harasses a young American kid, who comes into contact with the Digimon Adventure 02 cast. They come together to fight this corrupted Digimon, finally freeing it from the virus that took control.

The vibe of this movie is really weird, and it almost feels “liminal” in some instances, with it being set in remote locations and Wendigomon in general feeling eerie and unsettling. However, the action is also really good and the “golden” evolutions which debut in this firm are really cool. Some of these Digimon later re-appear in the Digimon Tamers show, but there’s no relation aside from species.

## Conclusion

Even though the western edition of Digimon: The Movie was a cobbled-together film made up of several short films with heavy editing, I still remember ir very fondly. From the weird but strangely cool soundtrack to the incredible animation and art style, there’s lots to love in this movie.

Also, Smash Mouth.
