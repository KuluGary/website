When I was a kid I had a Playstation 1, and I loved it. I spent a lot of time playing games, such as Crash Bandicoot 2: Cortex Strikes Back or Monsters Inc. Scream Team. Another one I spent an ungodly amount of time on is Digimon World, the original.

{% image category, postSlug %}
cover.png | I had the European version, but the Japanese cover art goes hard.
{% endimage %}

Digimon World is an infamous game for being cryptic with its game mechanics. The core loop of the game is a mix of a JRPG and a tamagotchi. You have to train your partner so it becomes stronger and evolves into more and more powerful forms. You also have to care for it, such as healing it when sick, feeding it when hungry, and letting it sleep when tired.

The issue is, the game is very unpredictable. It will not tell you which stat requirements you need to progress to the next evolution stage, and because of that more often than not you will be stuck… with Him.

{% image category, postSlug %}
numemon.png | I've seen this guy enough for a lifetime.
{% endimage %}

Whenever you have too many “care mistakes” (not feeding you Digimon, letting it poop on the ground, not letting it sleep…) your Digimon will evolve into Numemon. This little slime has bad stats and a terrible moveset, so if you try to tackle the challenges of the game with it you will die… a lot.

If you play this game as a kid you will likely want to get a MetalGreymon instead, because it’s all over the opening cinematic and promotional artwork. Well, good luck with that… The requirements are strict, and most likely you will not be lucky to match them on your first try.

{% image category, postSlug %}
metalgreymon.png | Such a cool-looking monster.
{% endimage %}

But all this is not to say I hate the game. Actually, I like it a lot! I feel like it has a unique charm, both aesthetically and mechanically. It feels like one of those games where there would be a ton of urban legends… “Hey! Did you know you could get MetalEtemon if you open the mysterious door in the forest!?”… And I like those kind of games.

The complexity of the mechanics also feeds into the mystique of the world. There’s no easily discoverable “formula” to get the strongest monster every time. As a kid, you’ll trust you instincts and sometimes you’ll get an awesome dinosaur and sometimes you’ll get an undespowered slime. I think that’s kind of fun.

But even with all that, the main issue kid-me had with this game… Is that it’s impossible. I don’t mean it’s hard or confusing or the final boss is too challenging… I mean I could not complete it.

See, I had a PAL version of the Playstation 1. And… I got to this screen.

{% image category, postSlug %}
fortress.png | This screen haunts me to this day.
{% endimage %}

I didn’t have a player’s guide for this game, so I assumed I missed something. Maybe I was underleveled, or I missed a story flag, or maybe I needed a partner Ogremon to be allowed entrance into the fortress. By the end of it, both my mom and I must have spent *hours* on this.

The thing is…

This game’s bugged.

It must’ve been 10 or more years later, when I had access to the Internet and one afternoon I remembered this cool game I played but couldn’t complete. I looked for it online, and there it was…

> Due to an error occurred during the localization of the game by Bandai in Europe, most PAL versions are impossible to complete. The error or bug is encountered in Ogre Fortress, where an Agumon blocks the entrance.
>
> – [Wikipedia](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digimon_World#Edición_PAL)

So it could not be completed. When I read it I had a feeling of “what! I wasted so much time”, but also, a slight vindication. It’s not that *I didn’t complete it*, it’s that *it could not be completed*.

Years later through [alternative means](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_piracy) I finally completed it and I loved it. The final areas of the game are amazing and the final boss is such a cool concept I still think about it to this day.
